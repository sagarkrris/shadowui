AlgoMaster audit and implementation opportunities

14 October 2026 · Compared with the current InterviewIQ / ElevatePrep source in shadowui.

Recommendation: improve discovery and reader usability first. Our code already contains learning roadmaps, notebooks, visual labs, company prep, AI mocks and Java build practice. The largest useful new capabilities are contextual annotations, chapter listening and a broader executable concurrency/LLD curriculum.

Verification scope and results

Checked all 64 unique HTTP destinations exposed by the homepage, its expanded menus, footer and FAQs, plus one mailto link. All 41 internal destinations returned HTTP 200 after redirects and were opened in the browser. Fifteen external destinations returned 200; eight LinkedIn links returned 999 and remain unverified. HTTP 200 alone does not prove that a protected workflow works.

This is a homepage/navigation audit, not a recursive audit of thousands of lesson, roadmap, problem or animation links. Signed-in dashboards, saved notebooks, leaderboards, paid chapters, AI responses, code execution, checkout and native mobile apps were not exercised. No account was created, no payment made, and no forms submitted. External 200 results establish reachability only. Email syntax was checked; delivery was not tested.

Menus and controls inspected

  • Learn: DSA, system-design fundamentals/interviews, LLD, concurrency, behavioral; Java, Python, C++, C#, Go and Git; all courses.
  • Practice: DSA patterns, system design, concurrency, LLD and company problem lists.
  • Interview: coding, system design and LLD mock entry pages.
  • Resources: animations, resume builder, roadmaps, LLD library, curated engineering blogs, GitHub and upcoming courses.
  • Compact navigation: all four categories expand, plus Newsletter (all/premium articles) and My Learning (dashboard/notebook). Desktop dropdowns were also opened at 1440 × 900; the temporary viewport was reset.
  • Account and support: login modal exposes Google, GitHub and email options. Streak calendar opens. All eight FAQ accordions expanded. All seven homepage learning-tool tabs selected and exposed demo panels; their examples are not proof of a working paid feature.

Observed limits and inconsistencies

  • Global Search opens with filters for courses, lessons, content, practice, animations and articles, but reported “Search is unavailable right now” both initially and for “java”. Treat this as an audit-session observation, not a confirmed outage for every user.
  • The homepage newsletter embed continued showing “Loading newsletter”. The newsletter site itself returned 200.
  • Dashboard, notebook and leaderboard redirect to login. Resume builder renders, but creation/import controls are disabled until sign-in.
  • Footer Practice → System Design and Low-Level Design lead to interview lists, while the Practice menu leads to executable practice lists. Both URLs work; the label implies different activities in different locations.
  • Several course roots redirect to their first chapter; /resume redirects to /resume/build. These are expected working routes, not broken links.

Build order

  1. Shared navigation and unified catalogue search.
  2. Reader preferences and searchable notebook.
  3. One original concurrency practice collection, with real checks and transparent runtime availability.
  4. One-course narration pilot and contextual highlights.
  5. Resume composition after the learning flow is cohesive.

Keep our production-debugging cases, Symptoms Atlas, tiny-system projects and evidence-based interview coaching prominent. Broad multilingual course expansion, public leaderboards, paid subscriptions and native mobile apps need separate product and infrastructure decisions. Prefer a responsive web experience and measured learner demand first.
